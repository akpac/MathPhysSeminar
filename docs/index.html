Welcome to the USN-TNM MathPhys Seminar Webpage!

This is a new seminar series under the Faculty of Technology, Natural Sciences and Maritime Sciences at the University of South-Eastern Norway, that is USN – TNM. The idea is to give blackboard (whiteboard) lectures in three of the campuses at USN: Kongsberg, Porsgrunn, Bakkenteigen (Vestfold). The date and speaker for seminar nr. n+1 will be decided at seminar nr. n. The time will always be Wednesdays 14.15 – 16 and the lectures should be scientific in mathematics, physics, or mathematical physics. This seminar can be viewed online, to obtain the link to join the seminars, please contact one of the organizers.

Upcoming Seminar

  • Friday 05.06.2026 at 14:15 USN, Campus Vestfold (Bakkenteigen)

    • Seminar by Nachiket Jhala (USN)

    Quantum States in Non-Commutative Harmonic Analysis

Abstract: By taking inspiration from the need for a unifying language producing quantum to classical transitions, while preserving details like positivity of states under the transition, we arrive at the non-commutative Fourier analysis program suggested by Korbicz in his PhD thesis. Commutative Fourier transforms are used in studying classical states, and non-commutative harmonic analysis generalizes these concepts to the non-abelian groups and the operator algebras to study quantum states. We study harmonic analysis on compact groups, using their representations and the Peter-Weyl theory to explore non-local correlations in quantum states. We also study separability criteria for quantum states studied in this language in terms of separability of functions on compact groups as suggested in works of Korbicz et al. This construction gives a natural quantization procedure by non-commutative harmonic analysis over Heisenberg - Weyl group, which is then compared with Wigner-Weyl quantization. Also, we study explicitly the quantum to classical transition of states represented in this language. If time allows, we will also talk about the extension of this language to compact quantum groups developed by Korbicz, Lewenstien, Wehr. One important notion, from physical point of view, is positive definiteness of elements of quantum group which is used to define the representation of quantum states in the language of compact quantum groups.
